## Editorial policy

Do not include fictional-company, fictional-publication or non-affiliation disclaimers in the reader edition.

Suggest the dispenser's disturbing corporate practices through euphemisms and omissions. Do not describe its lethal
mechanism or bodily processing. Never mention the viewer's screen or other fourth-wall-breaking details.

Write for people who love games. Assume no knowledge of game design or development. This rule applies to the cover,
contents, profiles, headings, captions, sidebars, credits and PDF metadata, not just body paragraphs. Describe what
readers can see or experience. Player terms such as health, attacks, checkpoint and reload are fine.

Keep sprite-sheet terminology, atlas details, frame counts, build IDs, runtime/integration status, collision rules,
validation notes and agent/process commentary out of the publication. Do not disguise technical explanations with new
headings: remove row/column/frame-count descriptions. Keep technical records in internal manifests and this README. The
`editorial_guidance` object in the JSON is internal guidance and is not rendered.

Remove tentative captions such as “Preview artwork,” “An idea we're exploring,” and “This account is still taking
shape.” Keep production status and image provenance in internal records. Picture captions should describe the subject,
action, atmosphere or useful player information. Do not discuss earlier versions, development history, prototype status
or production progress in the magazine. Never imply unfinished encounters are playable.

Actual unresolved editorial placeholders must use `TODO: <specific missing item>` in a conspicuous high-contrast box
with bold type at least 18 pt. Allow enough space to prevent clipping. Never hide TODOs in small italic captions or
vague caveats. Existing artwork does not need a TODO merely because gameplay remains unfinished.

Keep future plot spoilers, creature-origin explanations and boss solutions out of promotional copy. Preserve approved
profiles and lore, current names, and honest qualifications for unsettled stories. Preserve historical screenshots and
earlier editions. Request new illustrations through Bestiary / Art. Do not change mechanics or canonical creature
records while editing the magazine.

Extract the complete PDF text and audit it for development jargon, including bookmarks and metadata. Render and visually
inspect every affected page after rewriting; check that shorter copy still fits the layout naturally.

Follow the writing-style guidance in `../../AGENTS.md`. Prefer natural, varied sentences and specific observations
rather than repeated paired fragments or generic dramatic contrasts. Review headings, captions and body copy for
repetitive rhetorical patterns before publication.
