IN THIS ISSUE
TONY
THE EXPERIENCED HAND
Tony comes from four generations of
plumbers and has spent decades installing,
building, and keeping essential systems
running. He takes the work seriously and
makes time to teach Sean properly. A
difficult job won't put him off. A large spider
might slow him down a little.
TRADE ROOTS
Plumbing runs in Tony's family.
He brings that accumulated
knowledge to the job, along with
the patience to explain what he's
doing. Sean has a great deal to
learn, and Tony intends to give
him the chance.
TOOL TALK
Tony's pressure gun fires
quickly, so keep your aim
steady and give yourself room
when the creatures close in.
Sean's heavy plunger launcher
hits hard. Make each shot
count, and leave yourself room
while he reloads.
SEAN
THE APPRENTICE
Sean wandered in looking
for work and somehow
ended up on Tony's crew.
He's willing to help, although
it's worth checking what he's
holding before asking him to
hand you a tool. Tony is
showing him the ropes.
There are quite a few ropes.
Sean arrived without much
knowledge of plumbing.
Tony took him on anyway.
For now, enthusiasm is
doing a lot of the work, and a
patient teacher is keeping
the rest moving.

COMPANY BUSINESS
THE BOSS IS WATCHING.
BILLY-JOE
CALL HIM BJ.
Billy-Joe runs Mainframe
Plumbing & Supply from a
beige server rack. Every
plumbing company and
distribution warehouse in
the country answers to him,
so asking for a different
manager won't get you very
far. Tony and Sean get their
assignments from his green
screen. BJ has plenty of
work for them and very little
interest in hearing why it
might be unpleasant.
BJ speaks calmly, keeps the
jobs coming, and expects
his employees to get on with
them. If you're hoping the
smiling face means he's
concerned about your
welfare, you may want to
ask a more specific
question.

THE SPARE EMPLOYEE DISPENSER
Mainframe Plumbing &
Supply would like you to
know that arrangements
have been made. The Spare
Employee Dispenser stands
ready between service calls,
its glass front offering a
reassuring view of the
company’s preparations.
There is rust around the
hinges and something
familiar about the occupants.
The accompanying leaflet
describes them as reserve
personnel. It asks employees
to respect their rest and
directs all further questions
to the same leaflet.
The leaflet is particularly
clear on continuity of service.
Your assignments will remain
on the schedule, your tools
will still be required, and your
name will stay on the rota.
Several paragraphs explain
how much the company
values an experienced
employee. The section
headed “What this means for
you” refers back to those
paragraphs. We would have
preferred a little more detail,
but the cabinet does appear
to be well stocked.
The company asks that reserve personnel not
be disturbed.

CREATURE FIELD GUIDE / 01-03
01 / FLUSHFIEND
This bad-tempered fixture
has burning red eyes under
a lid-like brow, ceramic
teeth around its bowl, and
short pipe legs. It patrols
until a plumber gets too
close, then closes the
distance with a hop. The
service appointment is
unlikely to go as planned.
SERVICE TIP
Look for pipe legs, red
eyes and teeth around
the bowl. If it starts
following you, the
appointment has moved
beyond routine
maintenance.
Porcelain chips away until the fixture comes apart.
02 / THE HALLOWED WIDOW
For years, the worst thing
about a basement spider was
losing sight of it. Then one
sprouted wings and apparently
started breeding. The
Hallowed Widow is a huge,
hairy nightmare with ivory
fangs, white feathers, and a
golden halo - every reassuring
detail attached to the wrong
animal. Early witnesses
watched the floor. Later
witnesses learned to watch
the ceiling, too. Nobody
knows where the wings came
from. Religious and
non-religious people alike
have expressed concerns
about what this implies for
heaven.
Tony: "I was hoping spiders went somewhere else when they died."
03 /
GRATECRAWLER
WORKING NAME
A rusty drain strainer sits in
this sludge creature’s back,
above a mouth full of porcelain
teeth. It climbs from a grate
and patrols the floor. Watch its
hands: it pauses, faces you
and throws a ball of muck
before moving on.
Service tip: Move as its hand
rises. The muck arcs toward
where you were.

THE INSPECTION CONTINUES
04 / SPUTTERVALVE
That angry face belongs to a
pressure valve bolted firmly in
place. Its outlet spits violet shots
without a charging warning. It
cannot follow you, but it can aim at
you, so standing in front of it is still
a poor idea.
Service tip: Keep moving past the outlet. Don’t wait for a
charge warning before dodging.
05 / GNATASTROPHE
A maintenance crew found
Gnatastrophe circling a blocked
vent with the persistence of aircraft
waiting for landing clearance. They
have since been spotted throughout
the sewers, flying in close formation
toward no particular destination.
Their buzzing carries impressively
far for something with so little to report.
Plumbers and monsters alike suffer for
wandering into their flight path. Nobody
has identified a commanding officer,
although several flies appear to be
taking credit.
The flies drift without hunting. Crossing
their path hurts plumbers and monsters
alike.
06 / SKITTERLING
A cockroach crossing the
floor is bad enough.
Skitterling begins with
hundreds, crawling over
one another until the heap
takes on a shape you
recognize. Even then,
nothing holds still. Legs
and antennae keep moving
across its body, and every
part of the silhouette is
another insect trying to find
its place.
07 / THE CLOGFATHER
A colossal living fatberg of congealed grease,
wet wipes, roots, and things nobody should
have flushed. Wedged into the collection
chamber beneath headquarters, it heaves
against the pipework and spits the
accumulated consequences back at anyone
approaching.

THE SERVICE CALL HAS TAKEN AN UNEXPECTED TURN.
Easy Mode: Tony reports for work in a bonnet. The pressure gun is still required.
DRESS CODE
The basement still needs a plumber, even if
the uniform has become difficult to explain.
Easy Mode gives Tony and Sean a change
of clothes that Mainframe will probably
classify as protective equipment.
Keep some floor behind you when the
fixtures start moving. A bonnet will not
discourage a Flushfiend.
MIND THE
CURRENT!
The flush puts your tools out of
reach and leaves you
swimming among everything
somebody thought would fit
down a toilet. Watch the water
for incoming debris and keep
clear of the drain.
Once you are riding, the view
opens onto a channel of
rushing water and plumbing
hardware. Look ahead before
choosing where to cross. A
convenient-looking surface
deserves a second glance.
Tony and Sean have survived
plenty of unpleasant service
calls. Neither packed for this
one.
The flush: incoming debris has a
warning before it lands.

company is unlikely to reimburse
him.
