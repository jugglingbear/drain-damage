# Page 9 — Half the water back

Draft for Sean's review, November 25, 2026. Five panels. Not approved for artwork.

## Panel 1

Kitchen. The same caterer rinses the same pan under a healthy stream. Her phone rests on a dry counter beside
her, on speaker. Her shoulders have relaxed; the kitchen is working again.

**Caterer:** “Yes! That's much better. Whatever you just did, keep doing it.”

## Panel 2

Back in the mechanical room. The manager holds his phone on speaker so Tony and Sean can hear.
Tony points to the kitchen takeoff BEFORE the closed blue valve; the upstairs branch continues past that valve
and the low physical gauge. Frame the actual junction clearly enough to follow without a schematic inset.

**Tony:** “The kitchen comes off here, before that valve. Opening it gave upstairs water again, but it fed the
leak too. Enough was escaping that there wasn't much left for her.”

## Panel 3

The manager has lowered the phone. He faces Tony, worn down rather than theatrically guilty. Tony listens.
Sean stays beside the controller; nobody touches the valve.

**Tony:** “Who shut it off in the first place?”

**Manager:** “Maintenance. They'd found the leak. I thought I could get the bride's mother's shower running
long enough for her to rinse her hair. Then the kitchen started calling, and I found the water, and… you saw the tape.”

## Panel 4

Tony speaks to the manager at eye level. The manager is listening now, not reaching for another quick fix.
Allow enough panel width for an ordinary conversation rather than compressing it into a tiny reaction inset.

**Tony:** “The kitchen's water is back. Upstairs stays off until we've repaired that pipe. I need to see where
all that water went before we put any more through it.”

**Manager:** “I'll tell the desk. And the kitchen.”

## Panel 5

The manager stands at the mechanical-room exit, phone in hand. Tony and Sean remain inside, ready to investigate.
Keep the door and exit consistent with the page 8 room reference. Sean's plunger remains securely stowed.

**Tony:** “Keep this area clear for us. I'll need you to check an upstairs tap when we're ready.”

**Manager:** “Of course. Call me whenever you need me. And thank you.”
