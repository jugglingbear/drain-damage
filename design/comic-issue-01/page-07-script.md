# Page 7 — The service call

## Panel 1

The applause has died away. A caterer holds the ballroom service door open with her hip.
Her hands and forearms are covered in soap. She can see Tony. The demolished restroom is around the corner.

**Caterer:** “Can somebody get me a sink that works?”

## Panel 2

At the kitchen sink, a thin stream runs over one of the caterer’s hands. Sean offers her a clean towel.

**Tony:** “When did it get this bad?”

**Caterer:** “I washed my hands when the cake came in. Had plenty then. A few minutes later I couldn’t rinse a pan.”

## Panel 3

The manager has been listening from the kitchen doorway. This time he volunteers the information.

**Manager:** “That was me. About the time the cake came in, I opened a valve downstairs.
The bride’s mother stormed into my office with shampoo still in her hair, demanding I get the water back on. I thought
I’d found a way to get the upstairs taps running again.”

## Panel 4

Tony puts his light back on his belt. The manager looks tired enough to tell the truth.

**Tony:** “Did opening it help?”

**Manager:** “Upstairs? Yes. Then I came back through here and found water coming out of the service cupboard.
That’s when I got the tape.”

**Tony:** “Okay. Show me which valve.”

## Panel 5

On the service stairs, the manager is a landing ahead. Sean and Tony carry their gear. Tony keeps his voice low.
Show this beat in two adjacent subpanels: 5a is Sean’s question in close-up; 5b is Tony’s reply in the wider
stairwell view. The current proof crops the same rough for both. Keep Tony’s two sentences together in one
balloon, clearly attributed to him, and retain the manager farther down the stairs.

**Sean:** “I thought you were going to give him hell about the tape.”

**Tony:** “You saw his face. Took some guts to tell us what he did in front of his staff.”

**Tony (continued):** “Once the water’s back, I’ll take him aside and show him what to do next time.
He deserves to have that conversation in private.”
