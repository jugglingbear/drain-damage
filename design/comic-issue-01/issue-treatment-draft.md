## The plumbing arrangement

One shared incoming supply divides before the blue valve. The kitchen stays on one branch; the blue valve isolates
an upstairs guest-room branch. The physical gauge and electronic sensor are downstream of that valve.
The damaged pipe in the service cupboard belongs to this upstairs branch. Escaped water runs down the service
chase into a basement collection trench. This is the same leak, not a second unexplained broken pipe.

```text
Shared incoming supply
    +-- kitchen taps
    +-- blue valve -- gauge + sensor -- upstairs guest-room branch
                                           |
                                      damaged cupboard pipe
                                           |
                                      service chase
                                           |
                                      basement trench/drain
```

The upstairs branch had been isolated after the leak was noticed. Its closed valve explains the upstairs outage.
The manager reopened it when the bride's mother complained. Upstairs briefly improved from no water to usable
flow, but reopening also fed the damaged pipe. As the leak worsened, that added demand reduced pressure and flow
available from the shared supply, leaving the kitchen struggling and eventually affecting upstairs again.
The manager's duct tape did not repair the pipe. Do not introduce a secret bypass or another valve to explain this.

Page 8's shutoff stops new supply to the leak and upstairs branch. The downstream gauge falls as the branch loses
water through the existing leak. The kitchen, upstream of the shutoff, can recover. Water already in the trench
remains trapped by an obstructed drain. Its level does not keep rising without a shown new source.

This is a plausible story arrangement, not a calculated design for a real hotel. Leaks and simultaneous demand
can reduce available pressure/flow; exact rates and pressures are deliberately unspecified.
See [Thames Water's pressure guidance][pressure-reference] and [leak identification guidance][leak-reference].

### Page 13 — It attacks; they test its defenses

route in the drawing rather than teleporting it. Both heroes counterattack, but the loose outer mat absorbs or
redirects their blows. They learn why simply hitting harder will not finish the job.

### Page 14 — Sean is caught

Sean commits to keeping the creature away from Tony or their exit. It coils around his upper body and covers his
mouth and nose. He struggles purposefully to create breathing space while Tony moves to help. Keep the suffocation
beat brief and frightening; no long speech while Sean cannot breathe. His commitment creates the opening for the

### Page 15 — Rescue costs them ground

Tony breaks the hold and frees Sean, but gives up their favorable position or access to a tool to do it. Choose and
show that cost during panel scripting. Sean recovers and immediately contributes again. The creature's movement
partly lifts its mat from the outlet: trapped water can begin draining, making its compact central knot visible.
The blue valve remains closed; no new supply or unexplained pumping causes the water level to change.

### Page 16 — A meaningful hit

Their coordinated attack exposes and damages the central knot. Give the apparent breakthrough a short reaction
beat so the reader can believe they have found a winning approach. The hit permanently changes the creature;
record the damage and what has been stripped away. It does not reset to an intact opponent in the next panel.

### Page 17 — The reversal

The injured creature sheds its bulky outer mat and lunges with its denser core. Earlier movement should establish
that the outer strands can separate, so the reversal follows from the hit rather than introducing an arbitrary
power. The reduced mass makes it more mobile, not healed or stronger in every respect. It gets between both heroes
and their established exit. Show a concrete immediate threat to both, with their earlier positions and tool access
accounting for why they cannot simply step away or repeat the previous attack.

### Page 18 — A shared, deliberate risk

Sean deliberately catches and holds the exposed core long enough to create an opening for Tony's jetter. Tony must
wait for Sean's positioning and a clear shot rather than simply fire harder. Sean's strength and timing and Tony's
control are both necessary; neither could finish alone. The core breaks apart, leaving inert hair, loose cable and
hotel debris. Preserve believable grip, contact, tool state and escape geography in the roughs.

Establish the jetter's available water/charge before the fight; it cannot depend on reopening the damaged upstairs
branch. Confirm this against approved equipment references rather than inventing an upgrade. Keep dialogue sparse
during impacts, with fuller conversation reserved for breathing room and aftermath.

## Setup and payoff audit

- Blue valve: manager's mistaken reopening → Tony localizes and isolates the leak → controlled reopening after repair.
- Failing kitchen tap: shared supply loses flow → kitchen recovers when upstairs is isolated → both branches tested.
- Wall rushing: locates the leaking branch → shows the route to the trench → remains absent after repair.
- Stale screen: Sean recognizes a discrepancy → remote reassurance rejected → physical verification and defect report.
- Hair and wiring: obstruction retains escaped water → creature reveal and physical fight; origin remains ambiguous.
- Tony's empathy: manager volunteers useful facts → Tony keeps his promise to teach privately.
- Sean's skills: practical kindness, technical diagnosis, combat strength and checking the report all affect events.
- BJ's priorities: premature closeout pressure → omission in report; suspicion, not a revealed explanation.
