Recommendation: keep Tony and Sean’s unique weapons, make both responsive, replace repeated stop-and-clear waves with forward-moving encounters, and finish with a playable Clogfather. Prove the combat in a short opening section before rebuilding the whole level.

Consolidated from Level Design, Game Mechanics, Art and Storyboarding. Prototype 43 / 0.43.0 remains the playable build. Sean approved the recommended faster opening trial and sewer-system visual direction. Numerical settings are trial values, not final balance; new species, pickup details and dialogue remain provisional.

1. Faster kills and a more responsive Sean

Your report of a long wait between useful Sean shots matches a real design problem. The configured cooldown is one second; missing once, needing a second hit and waiting for projectile travel can make the exchange feel much longer. Tony currently needs seven hits for a full-health toilet.

At 240 pixels, a 24-HP enemy would take about 0.48 seconds for Tony or 0.70 seconds for Sean, including travel and assuming both hits land. Those are calculations, not playtest measurements. Bosses are explicit exceptions to the 1–3-hit rule. Avoid ordinary “elites” in the first comparison.

Alternatives: give both starters identical 12-damage / 0.18-second cadence if you prefer identity entirely through weapon behavior; or use a stronger contrast of Tony 12 / 0.20 and Sean 21 / 0.35. The recommended middle option keeps the original flavor with much less waiting.

2. Continuous action with room to react

Replace six mandatory clear-all stops with continuous finite encounters, one deliberate machinery set piece and a boss lock. Missed enemies behind the camera must retire without being counted as kills or blocking completion. This requires changing progression logic, not merely adding enemies.

Opening trial: 12 enemies across 20 seconds

Eight runners, one elevated shooter, one emerging threat and two flyers, plus a reachable airborne tool pickup. Start with forward arrivals, introduce a warned rear arrival, then layer an elevated attack and a marked grate entrance. The proposed 17-enemy opening is a later comparison, not the starting requirement.

The 17-enemy plan needs roughly 39 Tony hits or 34 Sean hits with the proposed tuning. At 70% accuracy, that occupies about 8.9 or 11.7 seconds of trigger time, before movement and target switching. It is plausible on paper but leaves Sean less slack. Test it instead of assuming it is fair.

  • Initial screen budget: six visible threats, two shooters, six hostile projectiles. Count entry warnings and contact enemies, not individual decorative flies.
  • After validation: brief peaks of eight threats and ten hostile projectiles, no longer than five seconds. Keep two shooters and no more than three independent attack patterns.
  • Visible warning of roughly 0.6–0.9 seconds for new shooters/emergers; no offscreen aimed fire, no landing on the player, and a reachable escape lane.
  • Use action beats followed by short easing/traversal sections. Delay excess spawns; never release a backlog as a sudden burst.

Level Design’s full-route sketch contains 90 ordinary enemies across six spaces (17, 15, 14, 18, 16, 10). Treat 80–100 as an authoring budget, not a quota. Keep the 9,600-pixel route as a planning envelope and measure a 4–6-minute human clear; shorten empty stretches instead of padding enemy health.

3. Real structures, several fighting lanes

Use pipe bundles, pumps and supported catwalks to offer upper and lower routes. Required rises stay at or below 60 pixels, gaps at or below 80, and landing widths at least 120, then validate with both heroes. Their current jump reaches about 86 pixels.

Catwalk tops need deliberate collision rules and clearance underneath. Background support columns can be decorative; clearly solid machinery blocks movement. Upper shooters must be reachable with the actual starter range and aiming angles.

All three are original concept illustrations, not game screenshots or approved new monster designs. Purple silhouettes, arrows and labels are planning annotations. Floor and viewer-side entries still need dedicated readable cues. Versioned images and exact generation prompts.

4. More behaviors without six new species

Keep Flushfiends as runners and the Gnatastrophe as a non-hunting environmental hazard. Evaluate the existing Hallowed Widow art for an aggressive flyer, which still needs gameplay implementation.

  • New stationary shooter: floor mounts and elevated perches, with a clear windup.
  • New emerger: floor and ceiling variants sharing behavior.
  • New skirmisher: quick traversal with background or viewer-side entrance animations.
  • An optional fourth new species only if the Widow cannot fill the aggressive flying role.

Left/right arrivals, floor/ceiling emergence and depth entrances are presentation methods, not six species. A background or viewer-side monster stays harmless and untargetable until it reaches its marked gameplay position and settles. Only one depth entrance at a time; large foreground silhouettes must not hide the hero. Names and designs need Art/Storyboarding review.

5. Unique defaults plus exclusive pickups

Keep an unlimited default weapon and add one pickup slot. A flying tool crate supplies something usable by the selected hero, avoiding useless other-hero drops in solo play. Fire cadence belongs to each weapon, so Sean can eventually equip a rapid weapon.

Initial concepts: Tony gets a pressure fan with three weaker bolts; Sean gets a short-range air ram with strong knockback. These are proposals, not approved weapons or available assets. Try a 20-second active-play duration, frozen during pause/dialogue/dispenser, with the default retained and the pickup lost on death. Duration versus ammunition, switching controls and replacement behavior remain decisions for the pickup trial.

6. The Clogfather is required

The redesigned level is not complete until both heroes can defeat a playable Clogfather. Toilet waves are no longer an acceptable final substitute. Use a low, reachable weak point, clear attacks, a broad fighting floor and a checkpoint immediately before the fight. Ordinary pursuit and lingering projectiles must not spill into the introduction.

Start with a measured 45–60-second fight target, allowing longer imperfect attempts. Mechanics proposes 1,080 HP for the faster starters: 90 Tony hits or 60 Sean hits, roughly 14.4 seconds of sustained exposure. That is a tuning candidate, not a committed stat. Do not reach a duration target through long invulnerability or inflated HP; both defaults must work without pickups. Begin with one primary pattern, no more than six hostile projectiles and one summon.

Completion must depend on confirmed boss defeat and stopped immediate hazards. Storyboarding’s proposed fact is “The Clogfather is down,” avoiding claims that all monsters are dead or plumbing is repaired. Draft endings have the other plumber on radio and BJ issuing another address. They remain unapproved dialogue; a usable exit and support character’s knowledge must be credible. No portal is assumed.

7. Build order and acceptance

  1. Combat comparison: trial damage/cadence; measure input-to-shot delay, hit counts, missed-shot recovery and firing while moving/jumping.
  2. 20-second playable opening: runner, shooter, emerger, one upper/lower structure and one pickup. Compare both heroes before increasing density.
  3. Full route: add flight/depth entrances, finite encounter scheduling, checkpoint-safe progress and approved art.
  4. Clogfather: attacks, art, phases, retry, defeat, appropriate ending and exactly-once mission completion.
  5. Playtest and polish: both heroes with defaults, controller readability, safe spawn lanes, performance/audio limits and a human full-route test. Build and capture actual screenshots with permission at each delivered iteration.

Engineering risks are manageable but substantive: current mission accounting assumes sequential clear-all encounters; current platform rectangles fill their whole footprint; enemy projectiles and pickup ownership need shared rules; the boss needs a real state machine. Automate those invariants, then use human playtests to judge pacing and fairness.

Recommended next decision

Approve the direction and the short opening trial: faster distinct starters, 1–3-hit ordinary enemies, supported upper/lower paths, 12 opening enemies before testing 17, exclusive pickups, and a mandatory Clogfather finale. Treat the numerical values as trial settings rather than final balance.
