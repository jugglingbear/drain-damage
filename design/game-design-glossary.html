Game Design Glossary

A shared vocabulary for discussing Drain Damage. Saved October 16, 2026.

These are practical working definitions. Some are common industry terms; others, such as “emerger,” are useful shorthand for our project. They describe roles and behavior, not necessarily separate monster species or features already implemented.

Enemies and hazards

Hunter
An enemy that actively seeks or pursues the player. Its movement responds to the player’s position. Example: a toilet monster that spots Sean and chases him.
Emerger
An enemy that enters the action by coming out of something: a floor grate, ceiling vent, background hatch or even the space in front of the screen. This describes its entrance; it could become a hunter or shooter after arriving.
Environmental hazard
A dangerous part of the space that the player must navigate around. It does not need to hunt the player. Examples: steam jets, electrified water or our wandering Gnatastrophe swarm, which can harm both plumbers and monsters. A hazard can be alive and destructible.
Runner
A ground enemy that moves through a combat lane. It may follow a set route or pursue the player; “runner” alone does not specify which.
Stationary shooter / perched shooter
An enemy that attacks from a fixed position. A perched shooter occupies an elevated position, encouraging the player to aim upward or take another route.
Flyer
An airborne enemy. Its flight may follow a pattern, wander or track the player; the term describes how it moves rather than how aggressive it is.
Skirmisher
A mobile enemy that attacks briefly, then retreats or repositions. It pressures the player through movement rather than standing still and absorbing shots.
Fodder
Relatively easy ordinary enemies that die quickly and keep action moving. They can still be dangerous in combination with other threats.
Elite
An occasional, clearly distinguishable enemy with greater durability or more demanding attacks than ordinary enemies. It should not be used as an excuse to make every monster slow to kill.
Boss
A major encounter with distinctive attacks, usually marking a level’s climax. The Clogfather is the required boss for our proposed first-level redesign.

Readable, fair action

Telegraph
A warning before danger: a windup, flashing grate, shadow, sound or landing marker that gives the player time to react.
Windup / attack / recovery
The preparation, dangerous action and settling period of an attack. Recovery often gives the player an opportunity to retaliate or reposition.
Attack pattern
A recognizable sequence or arrangement of attacks, such as a low projectile followed by a high one. Players learn patterns and find ways through them.
Combat lane / escape lane
A route through the action. A combat lane carries movement or attacks; an escape lane leaves the player a reachable way to avoid danger.
Play plane
The depth layer where normal gameplay collisions happen in our side-scroller. A monster can appear in the background before entering this layer and becoming dangerous.
Hitbox / hurtbox
A hitbox defines where an attack can connect. A hurtbox defines where a character can receive that hit. Neither has to match every visible pixel of the artwork.
Invulnerability frames (“i-frames”)
A short period when a character cannot take damage, such as the protection immediately after being hit.

Level flow and balance

Encounter
A designed stretch of action involving enemies, terrain and objectives.
Wave
A group of enemies introduced together or in a scheduled sequence.
Set piece
A specially arranged, memorable sequence, such as fighting through a pump failure while machinery opens a route.
Pacing / pressure and release
How intensity changes over time. Busy combat alternates with lighter movement or recovery so the entire level does not feel equally exhausting.
Threat budget
A limit on how much danger may be active at once: enemies, shooters, projectiles or independent attack patterns. Many visible creatures need not all attack simultaneously.
Encounter director
The game logic that schedules enemies and attacks while respecting progression, safe entrances and threat limits.
Fire cadence
The rhythm or interval between shots. A 0.25-second interval allows four shots per second while firing continuously.
Time to kill (TTK)
How long it takes to defeat a target under stated conditions. Distance, misses, armor and aiming can make real encounters longer than a simple damage calculation.
Damage per second (DPS)
A measure of sustained damage output. Equal DPS does not guarantee equal feel: one weapon may fire frequent light shots while another fires fewer heavy shots.
Pickup
An item collected during play, such as a temporary weapon, health or ammunition. An exclusive pickup can be equipped only by its intended hero.
Blockout / graybox
A rough playable layout made with simple shapes to test movement, routes and combat before investing in finished scenery.
Vertical slice
A small representative section combining the main systems well enough to judge the intended experience. “Vertical” refers to covering those systems, not to the level scrolling upward.
